## The part that matters: it refuses

Most of what this tool does is give you a calibrated interval. The part worth your attention is what it does when it should not give you one.

Two (scheme, size) cells have measured coverage so far below the 90% they advertise that the tool declines to answer for them at all: `w4a16|<2B` at 68.8<!-- claim: cell_coverage_pct::w4a16|<2B = 68.8312 -->% over 77<!-- claim: cell_rows::w4a16|<2B = 77.0000 --> scored rows, and `w8a16|>10B` at 73.7<!-- claim: cell_coverage_pct::w8a16|>10B = 73.7327 -->% over 217<!-- claim: cell_rows::w8a16|>10B = 217.0000 -->. It prints `INSUFFICIENT CALIBRATION`, shows the coverage it actually measured, and stops. A further 4<!-- claim: n_cells_blocked_from_tier_a = 4.0000 --> cells are barred from the top tier for resting on fewer than 3<!-- claim: min_cell_checkpoints = 3.0000 --> distinct checkpoints.

A risk estimate is only worth having if it tells you when not to trust it. The refused cells are exactly where a confident-sounding number would do the most damage.

## What it cannot do

- 0<!-- claim: intervals_excluding_zero = 0.0000 --> of 6 intervals exclude zero: it can never say a scheme *will* cost accuracy.
- 19<!-- claim: n_big_losses_below_floor = 19.0000 --> of 25<!-- claim: n_big_losses = 25.0000 --> observed losses worse than 3pp fell below their interval floor.
- It is built on configs that were published, i.e. that worked. See `BIAS_CORRECTION.md` for exactly how much that inflates the apparent coverage.
- It reads 2 inputs and nothing else about your model.
- **Every one of the 817<!-- claim: n_rows = 817.0000 --> calibration rows comes from a single publisher (RedHatAI) using a single toolchain (llm-compressor).** Holding out a model family tests transfer across *models*; nothing here tests transfer across *quantization practitioners*. Our own control arm is direct evidence that this matters: run by us rather than by Red Hat, the same scheme on comparable checkpoints came out 2.6<!-- claim: ctrl_ratio_x = 2.6015 -->x more damaging. Read these intervals as what a competent, well-tuned pipeline achieves -- not as what an arbitrary pipeline will.

## Evidence quality: what is clean and what is not

**Clean, and safe to rely on:**

- *Existence proof.* Deliberately-bad configs produce catastrophic damage: worst measured -39.5<!-- claim: adv_worst_delta = -39.5000 -->pp raw, 16<!-- claim: adv_bad_over_3pp = 16.0000 --> of 36<!-- claim: adv_bad_rows = 36.0000 --> faulted rows losing 3pp. This does not depend on any baseline.
- *Harness alignment.* Re-running MMLU at 5-shot with letter scoring reproduces published base accuracy to 47.20<!-- claim: s2_base_qwen05 = 47.2000 --> against 47.42<!-- claim: s2_pub_qwen05 = 47.4200 -->, and 59.57<!-- claim: s2_base_qwen15 = 59.5667 --> against 60.98<!-- claim: s2_pub_qwen15 = 60.9800 -->. Our measurements are on the same footing as the published corpus.

**Caveated, and not to be quoted as a bound:**

- *Excess over control.* Our own correctly-configured control is 2.6<!-- claim: ctrl_ratio_x = 2.6015 -->x more damaging than published w4a16 on closely comparable checkpoints (mean gap -3.78<!-- claim: ctrl_mean_gap = -3.7766 -->pp), because we calibrate on about 171<!-- claim: calib_ratio_x = 170.6667 -->x less data than production. So excess-over-control measures **bad recipe versus mediocre recipe**, not versus production. It is a *conservative anchor*, not a bound: we cannot demonstrate monotonicity on 6<!-- claim: s2_excess_n = 6.0000 --> rows from 2 models. *Not identical artifacts:* the published rows are the base checkpoints and ours the -Instruct variants; their base MMLU agrees to 0.37<!-- claim: ctrl_baseagree::Qwen2_5-0_5B = 0.3700 -->pp and 1.41<!-- claim: ctrl_baseagree::Qwen2_5-1_5B = 1.4133 -->pp, so they are comparable but not the same checkpoint.
- *No corrected point estimate.* The selection-bias correction moves by 5.70<!-- claim: s2_protocol_sensitivity = 5.7027 -->pp depending on which anchor set is used. We publish a range and refuse a single number.

## Helping close the gap

This tool's calibration is **static and drawn only from published model cards**. That has a known, measured limitation: published recipes are the ones that worked, and two (scheme, size) cells have too little data for the tool to answer at all. Real results from real runs are the fastest way to fix that.

If you evaluate a quantized model yourself, the numbers are worth sending back - especially for a combination the tool refused to answer, since those are the gaps.

**Report a result or just give feedback:** <https://gracejackson-sudo.github.io/quant-delta-predictor/>

The page has two sections. The first takes a measured result; the second is a free-text box you can send on its own, with nothing else filled in.

**What this currently is, precisely:** a hosted collection form and a local log of which combinations people ask about. Submitted results do *not* flow into the envelope automatically. A human validates them with `src/adversarial_schema.py` and re-runs the build. There is no backend, no ingestion pipeline and no telemetry; the request log stays on your own machine. Run `./.venv/bin/python src/rank.py --form-fields` to see exactly what is collected.

## Relation to prior work

[BenchPress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24020) (Zeng & Papailiopoulos, Microsoft Research, 2026) already published the core mechanism: predict an eval result, estimate whether to trust it, wrap it in a conformal interval, decide whether to skip the real run. Their method is more sophisticated than ours - a learned reliability estimator and a risk-normalised conformal wrapper, with public code and data.

This tool differs in three ways, none of which is a better mechanism:

1. **Domain and estimand.** Paired quantization deltas, not absolute benchmark scores. BenchPress contains no quantized checkpoints.
2. **It refuses.** Where measured per-cell coverage is poor or support is thin, it declines to emit a number. BenchPress flags risk but still returns an interval.
3. **It characterises its own selection bias** exactly, which BenchPress does not attempt. We have not *corrected* that bias - see `BIAS_CORRECTION.md` for the attempt that failed.

If you want per-model eval prediction, use BenchPress. This is for the narrower question of whether a given quantization scheme is safe enough to adopt without re-running your benchmarks.
